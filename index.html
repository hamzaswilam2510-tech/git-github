Muhammad Anwar el-Sadat was an Egyptian politician, military officer, and the 3rd president of Egypt from 1970 until his assassination in 1981. A member of the Free Officers who led the 1952 revolution, he succeeded Gamal Abdel Nasser and launched the Corrective Revolution, the Infitah economic policy, and a break with the Soviet Union. Sadat led Egypt in the 1973 Yom Kippur War, then pursued peace with Israel, culminating in the 1978 Camp David Accords and the Egypt-Israel Peace Treaty, earning the Nobel Peace Prize with Menachem Begin. His policies drew Western praise but intense Arab and domestic opposition, leading to Egypt’s suspension from the Arab League.
